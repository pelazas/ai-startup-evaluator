# Mission Clarity and Founder Execution

## Principle: Obsess over one painful problem
Great founders pick a user pain that is frequent, expensive, and emotionally sharp. They resist broad positioning early and instead become known for solving one painful workflow better than incumbents.

Early customer interviews should identify where teams already spend money or manual effort. If users are not trying to patch the problem today, urgency is probably weak.

## Principle: Ship learning loops, not perfect releases
In the first twelve months, startup speed comes from shorter feedback loops. Teams should design weekly release cycles tied to measurable hypotheses and post-release review.

Metrics should include activation, retention signals, and time-to-value, not only signups. The goal is reducing uncertainty quickly while maintaining trust.
